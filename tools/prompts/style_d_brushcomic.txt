Four characters from late-Joseon Korean classical heroic novels (yeongung soseol), whose stories are set in an idealized Ming-dynasty China. Everyone wears Ming-dynasty Chinese clothing and armor, NOT Korean hanbok. They stand side by side, full body, facing the viewer, evenly spaced, same scale, on a plain background. From left to right:
1) The young male hero, about 17, son of a loyal noble family, bright determined eyes, dark-gold scale armor over a crimson robe, helmet with a red tassel, holding a long straight sword.
2) The young female hero disguised as a man, about 17, the supreme commander of the army, slender and composed, sharp intelligent eyes, beautiful but androgynous, silver-white scale armor over a deep indigo robe, commander helmet with a white plume, holding a small triangular command flag, a sword at her waist.
3) The old Taoist master who rescues and teaches the hero, long white beard and eyebrows, kind eyes, grey Taoist robe with a crane pattern, holding a horsetail whisk, a small gourd at his belt.
4) The treacherous minister, in his 50s, sly narrow eyes, thin long moustache, black Ming official gauze hat with two side wings, purple official robe with a square rank badge, hiding a folded letter behind his back.
Each character must be instantly distinguishable by silhouette and main color. Absolutely no text, no letters, no labels, no seals, no signatures, no captions.
Art style: dynamic East Asian ink-brush comic illustration in the tradition of classic Korean historical manhwa: bold expressive black brush strokes with dry-brush texture, strong gestural lines and heroic poses, mostly black ink on warm off-white paper with a single accent color of vermilion red plus light grey washes, energetic and cinematic.
